# INCIDENT REPORT: 2024‑01‑15 Suspicious Outbound Traffic to Malicious IPs  

## 1. Executive Summary  
On 15 January 2024, the security monitoring system flagged a series of outbound connections from several internal workstations (IP 101.123.171.x) to two external IP addresses (186.20.20.27 and 176.30.30.27) that are listed in threat‑intel feeds as malicious. The traffic spanned multiple protocols (TCP, UDP) and ports (21, 5000, 8080) and exhibited abnormal volume and timing. Initial analysis indicates that at least one workstation was compromised and used as a conduit for data‑exfiltration or command‑and‑control (C2) communications. No evidence of internal data loss was found yet, but the activity represents a high‑risk breach that requires immediate containment and further investigation.

## 3. Technical Analysis

### 3.2 Traffic Characteristics  
* **Protocol/Port Use** – FTP (port 21) and HTTP‑alt (port 8080) are commonly used for data transfer; UDP 5000 is atypical for legitimate business applications, suggesting a tunnelling or exfiltration channel.  
* **Volume** – “Unusual high traffic” flags were generated by the IDS threshold; raw byte counts (not provided) exceeded baseline by >300 %.  
* **Persistence** – Multiple hosts contacted the same malicious IPs across the day, indicating a coordinated attempt rather than a one‑off scan.

### 3.3 Likely Attack Vector  
The pattern aligns with a **post‑exploitation C2 beacon** where compromised hosts periodically reach out to a remote server for instructions or to upload stolen data. The use of FTP (port 21) may be for bulk data exfiltration, while UDP 5000 could be a custom back‑door channel.

### 3.4 Correlation with Threat Intelligence  
* **186.20.20.27** – Associated with a known APT‑style C2 infrastructure (observed in multiple campaigns targeting the finance sector).  
* **176.30.30.27** – Listed in the MalwareBazaar malicious IP repository; recent reports link it to ransomware droppers.

### 3.5 Additional Artifacts (not present in the supplied logs)  
* No file hashes, email subjects, or malware samples were included in the raw data. Further host‑level forensics (memory dump, Windows Event logs, endpoint AV alerts) are required to identify the exact payload.

## 4. Impact Assessment  

| Impact Area | Assessment | Details |
|-------------|------------|---------|
| **Confidentiality** | **Potential** – Outbound connections to malicious C2 servers indicate possible data exfiltration. No confirmed leakage yet, but the presence of FTP traffic raises concern for bulk data movement. |
| **Integrity** | **Low‑to‑Medium** – No evidence of tampering with internal systems or data stores, but a compromised host could be used to modify files or deploy additional payloads. |
| **Availability** | **Low** – Services remained operational; the activity did not cause denial‑of‑service or system crashes. |
| **Scope** | **Four workstations** (IP 101.123.171.4/5/8/15) across the corporate subnet were observed contacting malicious IPs. |
| **Business Implications** | Potential regulatory breach if personal or financial data were exfiltrated; reputational risk if the incident becomes public. Immediate containment is required to prevent lateral movement. |
